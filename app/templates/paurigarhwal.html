District Profile

Welcome to Pauri Garhwal

A beautiful district in Uttarakhand that connects the Gangetic plains with the Lower Himalayas.

Pauri Garhwal · Hills, valleys and Himalayan surroundings

Pauri Garhwal is located in the Garhwal division of Uttarakhand, India. Its terrain includes fertile valleys, terraced agricultural land, pine and deodar forests, hill towns and distant Himalayan views. The district headquarters is Pauri.

The district is valued for its scenic beauty, cultural heritage and spiritual importance. Major settlements and service centres include Pauri, Kotdwar, Srinagar, Lansdowne, Satpuli, Thalisain and Yamkeshwar.

Land & Climate

Geography and Climate

Pauri Garhwal lies partly in the Gangetic plain and partly in the Lower Himalayas, including the Shiwalik range. Its landscape varies from lower valleys to wooded mountain ridges and high viewpoints.

  • Coordinates: 29°45′–30°15′ N and 78°24′–79°23′ E
  • Neighbouring areas: Haridwar, Dehradun, Tehri Garhwal, Rudraprayag, Chamoli, Almora, Nainital and Bijnor district of Uttar Pradesh
  • Natural features: valleys, hills, forests, terraced fields and Himalayan viewpoints
  • Climate: warm lower areas, pleasant hill summers, cold winters and monsoon greenery

Seasonal Character

Summer is warm in the lower regions, while the hills remain relatively pleasant. Winter is cold and higher locations may receive snowfall. The monsoon season brings rain, mist and a vivid green landscape.

Past to Present

History and Heritage

The district’s story is part of the broader history of the Garhwal Himalayan region.

Garhwal heritage · Traditional mountain settlement and landscape
EARLY MEDIEVAL PERIOD

Katyuri Influence

Parts of the Himalayan region were associated with the Katyuri dynasty between about the 9th and 12th centuries.

15TH CENTURY

Rise of Chandpurgarh

Local principalities developed, and Chandpurgarh rose in importance under Jagatpal.

GARHWAL KINGDOM

Unification Under Ajaypal

Ajaypal united many smaller territories. The region came to be known as Garhwal, and the capital later moved to Srinagar.

1804–1815

Gorkha Rule and British Period

Gorkha rule lasted until 1815, when the British defeated the Gorkhas. Eastern Garhwal then came under British administration.

1960 & 1998

Modern District Formation

Garhwal district was divided into Pauri Garhwal and Chamoli in 1960. Rudraprayag district was created in 1998.

Garhwali Identity

People, Culture and Traditions

Pauri Garhwal · Landscape, people and community life

Pauri Garhwal has a rich Garhwali cultural identity. Garhwali is widely spoken in homes and villages, while Hindi is used extensively in public administration, education and business.

Language

Garhwali is the predominant first language in the district. Hindi is widely used in schools, offices and public communication. Nepali and Kumaoni are also spoken by parts of the population.

Folk Music and Dance

Folk traditions include jagars and mangals, along with dance forms such as thadya, chhapeli and virahi. These traditions are important during celebrations and social gatherings.

Festivals and Fairs

Navratri, Shivratri, Diwali and Holi are widely celebrated. Local temple fairs and melas also create opportunities for cultural expression and community participation.

Community Life

Village customs, local deities, family networks, agriculture and seasonal festivals continue to shape community life across Pauri Garhwal.

Mountain Cuisine

Traditional Food of Garhwal

Garhwali cuisine is shaped by local grains, pulses, seasonal vegetables and mountain agriculture.

Traditional Garhwali and Uttarakhand cuisine

Local food is generally simple, nourishing and strongly connected to the crops grown in mountain villages. Grains, lentils, dairy products and seasonal vegetables form the base of daily meals.

🌾

Mandua Roti

Traditional flatbread made with finger millet, an important mountain grain.

🫘

Bhatt Ki Churkani

A regional preparation featuring bhatt, a locally valued black soybean.

🍛

Chainsoo

A traditional roasted-pulse dish known for its rich and earthy flavour.

🥣

Gahat Dishes

Horse gram preparations commonly served as dal or in warming soup-style meals.

Everyday Food

Roti, rice, dal, leafy vegetables, milk, curd and ghee are common. Jaggery also has a place in sweets and in religious offerings at local shrines.

District Services

Administration, Education and Connectivity

Administration

  • District headquarters: Pauri
  • Division: Garhwal
  • Administrative structure includes tehsils, sub-divisions, development blocks and villages
  • Lok Sabha constituency: Garhwal
  • Important towns: Pauri, Kotdwar, Srinagar, Lansdowne, Satpuli, Thalisain and Yamkeshwar

Connectivity

  • Road transport through buses and taxis is the main travel mode.
  • Kotdwar serves as the principal railway station / railhead for the district.
  • Jolly Grant Airport, Dehradun, is the nearest airport.

Key Education Institutions

  • Hemwati Nandan Bahuguna Garhwal University, Srinagar
  • National Institute of Technology Uttarakhand, Srinagar
  • Veer Chandra Singh Garhwali Government Medical College, Srinagar
  • Govind Ballabh Pant Engineering College, Ghurdauri near Pauri

Economic Activities

  • Terraced agriculture, pulses, grains and seasonal vegetables
  • Fruit cultivation and horticulture in suitable higher areas
  • Tourism, education, government services and forest-related livelihoods
Explore & Experience

Tourism and Places to Visit

Pauri Garhwal offers viewpoints, temple trails, hill stations, forests and peaceful mountain travel.

Khirsu

Known for Himalayan views, orchards, forest surroundings and the Ghandiyal Devta temple.

Chaukhamba View Point

A scenic viewpoint near Pauri with views towards the Idwal Valley and Himalayan peaks.

Lansdowne

A peaceful hill station known for deodar forests, colonial heritage and quiet walking trails.

Kanvashram

A spiritual location associated with the traditions of Vishvamitra, Menaka and Shakuntala.

Temples and Spiritual Heritage

Major spiritual and religious places include Kandoliya Devta, Jwalpa Devi Temple, Kyunkaleshwar Mahadev, Tarkeshwar Mahadev, Binsar Mahadev, Ekeshwar Mahadev, Danda Nagraja Temple and Shoonya Shikhar Ashram near Kotdwar.

Travel Guidance

Visitor Information

Best Time to Visit

  • March to June: generally pleasant weather in hill areas.
  • September to November: post-monsoon greenery and clearer views.
  • December to February: cold conditions, with possible snowfall in higher locations.

Responsible Travel

  • Respect local customs, temple rules and village traditions.
  • Seek permission before photographing people or religious rituals.
  • Avoid littering in forests, viewpoints, temples and trekking routes.
  • Check weather and road conditions before travel during monsoon season.
