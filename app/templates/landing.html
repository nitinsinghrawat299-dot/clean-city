A town worth protecting

Perched at 1,765 metres in the Garhwal Himalayas, Pauri has been the administrative heart of the region since the British made it their headquarters in 1840 — a legacy that continued when it became the seat of the Garhwal Division in 1969. Long before that, this land was ruled by the Katyuri kings and later unified under the historic Garhwal Kingdom. Today it's a town where snow-capped peaks are visible right from the ridge, and where Garhwali culture and hospitality run deep.

Why people love Pauri

🏔️

Chaukhamba View Point

Just 4 km from the town centre, this viewpoint overlooks the Idwal Valley with unobstructed views of the Chaukhamba peaks — one of the closest genuine Himalayan panoramas you'll find this easy to reach.

🛕

Kandoliya Devta Temple

Set among pine trees, this is considered the guardian deity of Pauri. Every year its bhandara draws thousands of visitors from Pauri and surrounding villages.

⛩️

Kyunkaleshwar Mahadev

An 8th-century Shiva temple in Pauri's suburbs, with snow-laden Himalayan views from its courtyard — one of the town's oldest and most peaceful spots.

🥾

Khirsu & beyond

19 km away, the pollution-free hill town of Khirsu offers wide Himalayan views, while nearby Lansdowne makes Pauri a natural base for exploring quieter corners of Garhwal.

Small town, big names
